# Change how a space looks

Click the **gear** next to a space in the Vibespaces list (or the pencil at the top of the list, for the space this
window has open). A form opens with:

- **Name:** rename the space. Its taskbar pin keeps working.
- **Colour:** click a colour, or the picker for any colour you like. The preview shows how the window will look.
  You can also give the space its own **colour theme**, so it looks different all over.
- **Folders:** add another folder to the space, or remove one. Your files are never moved or deleted.
- **Taskbar icon:** a little window in the space's colour, a tile with its initials, or a picture of your own.

### When this space opens

The quickest start is a **preset**: pick one at the top of this section and click **Apply**.

- **AI assistants**, one each, with the assistant filling the window and a reset that tidies stray tabs:
  - **Claude vibe coder:** Claude Code as a full-window tab.
  - **Codex**, **Antigravity**, **Gemini Code Assist**, **Grok (AFK Pilot)**, **GitHub Copilot**, **Cline:** the
    assistant fills the window from the right side bar. If one starts in the left bar (Antigravity, Gemini and
    Cline do), drag its icon to the right side bar once.
  - Not installed yet? The preset says so and opens its page in Extensions.
- **Layouts:** **Classic editor** (files on the left, a terminal along the bottom) and **Clean slate** (just the
  editor).

Like how one of your spaces is set up? **Save this space's setup as a preset…** and it's offered whenever you make
a new space. A preset is only the panels, the window arrangement and the tab tidying, never a project's files,
terminals, web page or window position.

Or set each part yourself:

- **Open these files:** the files you always start with, like a to-do list or the main page.
- **Show these panels:** the file list, search, source control, and any panel your extensions add: AI assistants
  (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini, Copilot…), Docker, Python and so on. New ones appear here once installed.
- **Arrange the window:** show or hide the left side bar, the right side bar and the bottom panel. *Fill the window*
  makes the right side bar take the whole window, handy for chatting with an AI assistant.
- **Run in terminals:** commands to start, such as your website's preview server, each in its own terminal.
- **Open a web page:** for example `localhost:3000`, in your browser or inside VS Code.

**Try these steps now** runs them straight away, so you can check them without reopening the space.

**Where the window sits (Windows):** under *Arrange the window*, pick a spot: a narrow or wider column on the left
or right, a half, the centre, or full screen. The window moves there straight away, on whichever screen it's on.
For an exact spot, put the window where you like it and click **Use where this window is now**. Tick *Also move the
window there when the space opens* to have it land there every time.

**Get your layout back:** moved panels around, left the full-window assistant, or dragged the window off somewhere?
Press **Ctrl+Alt+Home** (or click the house button at the top of the Vibespaces list) and the space's panels, layout
and window position come back. Your terminals and web page are left as they are.

To have the reset tidy up too, set *Open tabs, when you reset* to **Close the ones that aren't part of this space**.
Settings pages and other stray tabs close, and the space's own files come back. Its files, the assistant tabs it
opens, pinned tabs and anything with unsaved changes are always kept. To use a different key, open *File > Preferences > Keyboard Shortcuts*
and search for *Reset to This Space's Layout*.

### For all spaces at once

Open **Settings** under the Spaces list in the Vibespaces side bar:

- **Colour these parts:** title bar, bottom bar, the icon bar on the left, a line over the open tab, the window border.
- **Strength:** *Bold* uses the full colour; *Subtle* uses a gentle tint of it.
- **Taskbar:** turn the separate taskbar buttons off or on, and choose the icon for new spaces.
